## Helping the immune system recognize cancer

How can we help the immune system recognize a tumor and mount a response against it? That question connects Genvira’s therapeutic programs.

### Engaging the tumor immune environment

[GVB-2030](@/programs/gvb-2030.md) is an investigational vaccinia-based immunotherapy for bladder cancer. It is designed to stimulate an antitumor immune response. One focus of our research is tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS), clusters of immune cells that can form in tumors. Published bladder cancer research has linked higher densities of mature TLS with better tumor response and longer event-free survival in patients who received checkpoint immunotherapy before surgery.[^platform-tls]

### Personalizing how the immune system sees the tumor

[GVB-2040](@/programs/gvb-2040.md) brings a patient-specific approach to the same goal. The preclinical cancer vaccine program uses information from a patient sample and tumor biopsy to tailor the vaccine to the patient’s human leukocyte antigen (HLA) profile. HLA helps determine which tumor antigens T cells can recognize.

### Begin with the vector.

We use vector engineering, in-house libraries, and AI/ML-guided design to explore genetic stability, payload design, and immune activity.

These choices create candidates that can be evaluated in the laboratory. Proprietary sequences and engineering methods remain outside this public overview.

---

### Understand performance.

Potency, durability, and immunogenicity assays help compare candidates and guide further development.

The goal is to connect vector design with measurable biological characteristics.

---

### Consider the path beyond discovery.

Process development and manufacturing considerations inform how candidates move toward preclinical and partnered development.

We consider production yield and scale-up early in vector design, with the goal of developing processes suitable for future manufacturing under Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP).
